With the rise of XR glasses like the XREAL One, you can have a personal cinema or a portable workstation anywhere you go with just your smartphone. But to drive that experience, you need a proper device that support such feature.

## The First Hurdle: Not All USB-C Are Created Equal

Before you rush out to buy a pair of XR glasses, it's crucial to know that not many smartphones support video output over USB-C. This feature is often exclusively reserved for the flagship devices.

Using Samsung Galaxy devices as an example, this feature is absent from the budget and mid-range lines like the A-series. You'll need a phone from the high-end S series, the productivity-focused Note series (now integrated into the S Ultra), or the innovative Z Fold series to get a signal to an external display. Even the capable Galaxy Tab A9+, which supports the DeX desktop environment on its own screen, lacks the ability to output to an external monitor via its USB-C port.

Samsung maintains an official list of compatible devices, which you can find right [here](https://www.samsung.com/ph/support/mobile-devices/how-to-use-a-samsung-usb-c-to-hdmi-adapter/).

If you're looking beyond Samsung Galaxy smartphones or tablets, the key is to find a device with a USB-C port designed to support  **DisplayPort Alternate Mode (DP Alt Mode)** to output video. This is an optional feature, meaning not all devices with a USB-C port have it. Yes, it's annoying, which is why [Microsoft is trying to stop this madness](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftusbblog/ending-usb-c%C2%AE-port-confusion/4410479).

Here's a breakdown of how different USB standards relate to DisplayPort output:

* **USB 3.1 & 3.2:** Many devices with these ports, particularly laptops and higher-end tablets and smartphones, support DisplayPort Alt Mode. However, it is not a mandatory part of the specification. You must check the device's specifications to be sure.

* **USB4:** The USB4 standard, introduced in 2019, mandates support for DisplayPort Alt Mode 2.0. This means that any certified USB4 port should be capable of video output. The DisplayPort 2.0 integration allows for very high-resolution and high-refresh-rate displays.

* **Thunderbolt 3 & 4:** Developed by Intel, the Thunderbolt 3 and 4 standards also use the USB-C connector and inherently include support for DisplayPort. Any device with a Thunderbolt 3 and 4 port will support video output.

Since this feature isn't a given on all devices (especially those prior to the widespread adoption of USB4), the most reliable method is to check the official product page or user manual for your device. Look for terms like "DisplayPort Alternate Mode", "DP Alt Mode", or "video output" in the description of the USB-C port's capabilities. Manufacturers often place a small DisplayPort logo (a "D" shape with a "P" inside) next to the port that supports video output. A small lightning bolt symbol next to the USB-C port also indicates that it's a Thunderbolt port, which will always support DisplayPort output.

With that in mind, I got my hands on the Samsung Galaxy S25 to test it out with the XREAL One XR glasses, and I found that they make a fantastic pair.

## The Out-of-the Box Experience: Welcome to DeX

By default, when you plug the USB-C cable from the Galaxy S25 into the XREAL glasses, it doesn't just mirror your phone screen. Instead, it launches **Samsung DeX**, a powerful feature that turns your phone into a full desktop computer.

The view inside the glasses transforms into a complete desktop-like environment, while your phone's screen becomes an intuitive touchpad for controlling the mouse cursor. You can easily connect a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse to complete the setup. In this mode, you essentially have a full laptop in your pocket alongside the large external screen provided by the XREAL glasses. It's a productivity powerhouse.

## The Simple Alternative: Screen Mirroring

But what if you don't need a full desktop? What if you just want to see exactly what's on your phone screen on the giant virtual display? You can switching to a simple screen mirror mode by stop the DeX mode.

### How to Switch From DeX to Screen Mirroring

1. While connected, swipe down from the top of your phone's screen to access the **Quick Settings panel**.
2. Tap the **DeX** icon to deactivate Samsung DeX.

Your XREAL glasses will now simply mirror your phone's display.

### How to Set Screen Mirroring as Default

If you find yourself primarily using the screen mirroring feature, you can change the default behavior when you connect the phone to external screen so you don't have to switch out of DeX every time.

1. On your phone, go to **Settings**.
2. Search for and select **Samsung DeX**.
3. Find the toggle for **"Auto start when HDMI is connected"** and turn it off.

With this setting disabled, every time you connect the XREAL glasses to your phone, it will start in screen mirroring mode. If you want to launch the desktop experience, you can simply tap the DeX icon in the Quick Settings panel to activate DeX mode.
