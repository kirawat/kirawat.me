RustDesk is an open-source remote desktop software that allows users to access and control computers and other devices remotely. Written primarily in the Rust programming language, it has gained popularity as an alternative to well-known commercial applications like TeamViewer and AnyDesk.

One of the key distinguishing features of RustDesk is its emphasis on privacy and control. Users have the option to use the public rendezvous/relay servers provided by RustDesk or to self-host their own server. This self-hosting capability gives users full control over their data, ensuring that their remote connections are private and secure.

## Negative Perceptions Surrounding RustDesk

### Lack of Transparency and Suspected Origins

Despite the company, Purslane Ltd., being registered in Singapore, many users have expressed concerns about its potential ties to China. These suspicious were fueled by:

* Early versions of the software reportedly used relay servers located in China, raising privacy concerns for some users.

* The lead developer's identity and the project's funding sources were not always clear, leading to speculation.

* Users reported that issues and questions raised on the project's GitHub page concerning its Chinese connections were sometimes deleted without explanation. This lack of open discussion was seen as a major red flag and led to accusations of a lack of transparency.

While the development team has since clarified that they are based in Singapore and have taken steps to be more transparent, these initial concerns created a lasting sense of mistrust for some potential users.
