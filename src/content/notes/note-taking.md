## Types of Notes

### Fleeting Notes

**Ephemeral vessels of ideation** — Temporary containers for your initial thoughts and ideas.

The purpose of fleeting notes is to quickly capture ideas, thoughts, or observations that come to you while reading, listening, and anything that comes to mind before it fades away. Whether it's a quote that resonates with you, a question that pops into your mind, or a sudden burst of inspiration. It is unstructured, informal, and meant to be temporary.

> "Because our brain works 24/7 and our thinking cannot be contained to a dedicated note-taking moment, we might take notes on the go. When you jot down the random ideas, thoughts and information that untimely flow through your mind, you are taking fleeting notes.
> 
> Fleeting notes are unstructured and unorganized notes. They serve as temporary holders of information for later use that you will need to organize into your system. In short, fleeting notes are temporary reminders of ideas."[^1]

### Reference Notes

**Constructing a verifiable compendium of facts** — Collection of information that is factual, accurate, and can be traced back to its original source for confirmation.

The purpose of reference notes is to store factual information, definitions, or data that you might need to reference later. It can be as simple as a few bullet points or a more structured outline. The important thing is to include enough information to identify the source and understand the key takeaways.

Reference notes act as building blocks for further exploration and systhesis. They save time by allowing you to quickly revisit information without having to reread the entire source material.

Think of them as your personal encyclopedia, always ready to provide accurate information when needed.

> "The technique of grouping information, organizing ideas into categories, and creating tags to help you find grouped information at a later stage is the art of reference notes. When we reference something, it is safe to say that the topic or idea we are writing about is part of a bigger topic or is information accredited to someone or someplace else."[^1]

### Literature Notes

**Extracting knowledge from external repositories** — Identifying, understanding, and capturing in your own words the key insights, arguents, and information from sources.

The purpose of literature notes is to summarise and distill key ideas or information from external sources such as books, articles, lectures, etc. These notes summarise they key points and insights from the material, transforming the author's words into your own understanding. It may include quotes or paraphrases with proper citations.

Literature notes help you deepen your understanding of the topic and develop your own unique perspective. They also serve as a starting point for the creation of [permanent notes](#permanent-notes).

> "Oftentimes when working or reading for leisure, there's a particular text we either wish to highlight or jot down for future reference. Literature Notes are the notes you make as comments to the text you have selected or marked down.
> 
> Taking literature notes adds context and helps you remember the thought you had when reading or writing down the information. The whole point of adding comments to the text you highlight is to help you summarize the point of the text, to include your thought and your key takeaways from it."[^1]

### Permanent Notes

**Weaving a bespoke tapestry of knowledge** — The ongoing, personal process of creating a unique, interconnected, and richly detailed structure of your own understanding by meticulously linking individual, atomic ideas and insights into a cohesive and evolving whole.

The purpose of permanent notes is to synthesise your own thoughts and insights based on the information you've gathered, represent your own understanding and interpretation of a topic. Its often linked to other notes to create a web of knowledge. It is the most refined and polished of the note types.

> "Permanent notes are stand-alone ideas that can be made without any direct context to other sourced information such as books, videos, or other available data.
>
> Permanent notes can be made as a recap or summary of the information just researched or learned, but can also be thoughts that popped into your brain while thinking over a myriad of information or while analyzing any given context.
>
> The aim of permanent notes is to process the notes you have made and analyze how they affect your interests, thinking, and research. You then cherry pick the notes that add value to your existing ideas and connect the new information to what you already know and have saved in your database."[^1]

### How These Notes Work Together

1. Fleeting notes are processed and transformed into literature notes or reference notes.

2. Literature and reference notes provide the raw material for creating permanent notes.

3. Permanent notes become the building blocks of your knowledge base, allowing you to develop original ideas and make connections between different topics.

## Note-Taking Style

There are three main note-taking style:

* **[The architect](#architect):** They enjoy planning, designing processes and frameworks, and need a note-taking tool that allows them to easily structure their ideas.

* **[The gardener](#gardener):** They enjoy exploring, connecting various thoughts together, and need a note-taking tool that allows them to easily grow their ideas.

* **[The librarian](#librarian):** They enjoy collecting, building a catalogue of resources, and need a note-taking tool that allows them to easily retrieve their ideas.

### Architect

The architect rely on hierarchical structure based on pages and categories. They need a bit of planning to decide whether an idea deserves its own category, its own page, or just its own block of content. But once they have gone through that decision-making and design process, they can keep re-using their templates for different workflows, which can greatly enhance productivity.[^2]

### Gardener

Gardeners prefer a non-linear approach to note-taking. They might start with a seed of an idea and allow it to grow organically, discovering the structure and connections as they progress.[^2][^3]

### Librarian

Librarians primarily use note-taking apps as a place to store and retrieve information they care about. Their ideal note-taking apps feature web clipping, rich formatting, file attachment, tagging, and a filtering system. These tools are all they need to quickly save and access what they need, whether it's some book notes, meeting notes, or a recipe.[^2]
